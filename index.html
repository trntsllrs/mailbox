When the Mailbox
Went Empty

PRIORITY MAIL // BY TRENT SELLARS

A polished khaki mailbox, stood post at the edge of his yard,
And every other morning, Old Pat would hand him a card,
A present from aunty Helen, a neighbour's wedding invite,
A reminder about the insurance, with a rate that wasn't quite right.

On Sundays, the local paper; on Wednesday, a Woman's Day,
On Friday, a pamphlet for groceries; but nothing on a Saturday.
Once a year, at Christmas time, he'd be dressed in a tinsel suit.
The mailbox, at the edge of his yard, stuffed full of festive loot.

He didn't mind the busy times, nor the weight of his usual duty,
He didn't mind that he had to hold, not keep, the passing booty.
He liked the puppy at his leg, that barked and wet his limb.
And too the pigeon on his head, that trod and cooed upon him.
